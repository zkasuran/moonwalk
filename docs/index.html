Nanopayments that cost
less than the call.

MoonWalk is the payment channel that makes a $0.001 call worth settling. The payer funds once with a signed authorization, then signs one cumulative voucher per call. The service redeems the whole batch in one transaction, so the fee is shared across every call instead of paid per call. Each person also gets a spend cap held in the contract, so one agent can serve a whole channel of people without anyone trusting the operator. The lifecycle

Sign → check → settle.

One endpoint offers both rails. The 402 carries the x402 requirements in PAYMENT-REQUIRED and the channel offer in X-CHANNEL-REQUIRED. The caller picks by which header it sends back.

01 · SIGN

A voucher, not a transaction

The payer funds the channel once with a signed EIP-3009 authorization, then signs one EIP-712 voucher per call. A voucher is a cumulative total for one person, so the newest one supersedes every earlier one and a lost voucher costs nothing.

02 · CHECK

Against the contract, not a config

Before the work happens the service checks the signature, the cumulative and that person's SpendGuard cap. It reads the cap from the contract, so refusing a call and failing to redeem it can never disagree.

03 · SETTLE

One transaction, every call

Once enough has accrued the service calls redeem with the newest voucher per person. One transaction, one USDC transfer, every call since the last redeem. The payer's own tx count stays 0.

1 wallet, N caps

each person capped in the contract

A subject is keccak256("discord:<guildId>:<userId>"), so SpendGuard holds a limit per person while the agent spends from one wallet. A voucher over someone's cap cannot be redeemed by anyone, the operator included. For the judges

Every question, answered.

The honest version, up front.

Is the money real?

Yes. Real USDC moves between two distinct wallets on Arc mainnet, status 1 on every receipt. You can click each hash on this page. They are permanent on-chain records that need no server to verify. In the mainnet proof run the channel was funded with $0.100000 and $0.030000 was redeemed to the service, which the panel above reads straight from the chain.

Why a payment channel and not just x402 per call?

Because the fee beats the price. One x402 settlement of a $0.001 call cost $0.001873 in gas on Arc, measured during the build. The channel spreads that cost: 30 calls redeemed in one transaction cost $0.006565 in total, which is $0.000219 a call. x402 is still the right rail for a first call or a one-off, so the same endpoint offers both. Those figures were measured on Arc testnet during the build and are labelled testnet on this page.

Is the payer really gasless?

On the happy path yes, measured rather than claimed: the payer's transaction count on Arc mainnet is 0 after a run that opened a channel, metered calls, settled them and closed. It signs the deposit, every voucher and the close. Anyone can submit those. In production the service does. One exception: if the service goes dark the payer has to send requestClose and withdraw itself, which needs USDC for gas.

Is the agent really deciding or is it scripted?

It is a real model tool-use call over a priced catalog. It can buy a tool, pick a different tool or answer for free and spend nothing. Ask it a greeting and it spends $0; ask for a live price and it buys one. The budget is enforced in code after the model picks, so a hallucinated number can never overspend.

Why does the agent show 0 on-chain transactions if it paid?

EIP-3009, the x402 "exact" scheme. The agent signs a transfer authorization off-chain; a facilitator broadcasts it and pays the gas. On every receipt the from is the facilitator, not the agent. The to is the USDC contract. The payer needs USDC, nothing else.

What is genuinely yours versus the Circle SDK?

The three contracts are ours: the channel, the cap enforcement and the registry, with 66 tests behind them. So is the Python chain package with its committed ABIs, the service-side rail that quotes and batches, the payer-side voucher client, the dual-rail 402 and the Discord surface. Circle's parts are USDC with EIP-3009 on Arc plus the x402 protocol plumbing. The settlement layer on top is the build.

What stops one person draining the agent's wallet?

SpendGuard, in the contract. Each person is a hashed subject with its own limit, lifetime or per window. A voucher that would push them past it makes redeem revert. The operator cannot override that at redeem time. It also fails closed: a guarded channel with no cap configured cannot spend anything at all. When the bot sees a cap is reached it refuses the call rather than quietly switching to the other rail.

Which Circle tools does it use?

The x402 exact scheme, EIP-3009 USDC on Arc, an embedded facilitator that settles in-process plus USDC-as-gas. The two-wallet split (agent payer, service facilitator) is what makes each settlement a real transfer between distinct parties, not a wallet paying itself. The channel adds EIP-712 vouchers and EIP-3009 receiveWithAuthorization for the deposit.

Has anyone actually used it?

The bot has been live and paying in Discord since early July. The channel rail proven here is new, so its evidence is the mainnet lifecycle run above and the panel that reads it live from the chain, not months of traffic. We report the real number, never an inflated one.

What are the limits you are not hiding?

If the service goes dark the payer reclaims the outstanding balance itself after the channel's challenge window, which costs it gas. The service trusts nothing but it does have to keep its vouchers: lose that store and it loses the right to collect. The Discord marketplace writes to the ServiceRegistry. The service wallet submits those transactions because a member has no wallet or gas, so a listing records the operator as its lister while payTo is the member's own address. The contracts are unaudited. The full list is in the README.
